# Adapting the course cleanly

This Starter separates four concerns that were mixed together in earlier
offerings:

1. **Capability:** what students should be able to do.
2. **Learning journey:** the sequence of examples, practice, and evidence.
3. **Theme:** the story or domain that makes the work meaningful.
4. **Operations:** how repositories are created, collected, and reviewed.

Change one concern without silently rewriting the others.

## Keep stable when possible

- the problem → plan → code → test → explain loop;
- weekly lecture, lab, project, and feedback rhythm;
- two units of increasingly independent programming;
- controlled transfer assessments;
- final project specification, incremental checkpoints, and demo;
- the staged AI policy.

## Safe places to make the course your own

- examples used in lecture;
- Unit 2 application domain;
- project themes and datasets;
- optional stretch goals;
- local meeting pattern and grade weights;
- supported editor or development environment.

## Before changing the sequence

Write down:

- the capability that moves;
- the prerequisite it depends on;
- the evidence that will demonstrate it;
- the lab that prepares students for that evidence;
- the later work that depends on it.

If those answers are unclear, experiment in a private term branch before
changing the reusable baseline.

## Theme guidance

Earlier versions successfully used a fictional company, an academy, and a
science-fiction foundation. Those themes increased energy but also increased
authoring and maintenance cost. Keep a theme only when it helps students
understand the data or problem. The baseline should remain teachable if every
proper noun is removed.
